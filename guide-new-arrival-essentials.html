New Arrival Essentials for Off-Base Life

The humidity, 100V, and typhoon gear families wish they'd bought in week one.

PR This guide contains affiliate links. If you book or buy through them, I may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. I only list things I'd actually point a friend to. Details

Off-base life in Okinawa comes with a few surprises the PCS briefing doesn't cover: humidity that grows mold on shoes in a week, 100V outlets, and typhoon season. Here's what new arrivals end up buying in their first month — all available on Amazon Japan, which delivers straight to off-base addresses (usually in a day or two) and can be browsed in English.

Tip: Amazon Japan uses your off-base Japanese address, not your APO box. Switch the site language to English from the flag icon at the top of the page.

1. Fight the humidity first

From the May–June rainy season through late summer, indoor humidity can stay high for weeks. Mold on closets, leather, and mattresses is one of the most common complaints from new arrivals in off-base housing.

💧

A compressor or hybrid dehumidifier

Run it in closets and bedrooms with the doors shut. Look for a model with a continuous-drain option so you aren't emptying a tank twice a day. Japanese models run natively on 100V.

🧽

Mold remover & prevention spray

Japanese bathroom and window-frame mold cleaners are strong and effective. Keep some on hand before it becomes a problem — and photograph any existing mold at move-in for your deposit.

See mold care products →

2. Make your U.S. appliances work

Off-base housing in Japan runs on 100V (Okinawa is 60Hz, same as the U.S.). Many U.S. 120V electronics work fine, but motors and heating elements — stand mixers, some coffee makers, power tools — can run weak or wear out faster. A step-up transformer fixes that.

🔌

Step-up transformer (100V → 120V)

Check the wattage on your appliance label and choose a transformer rated comfortably above it (for heating appliances, generously so). Plug shapes are the same Type A as in the U.S.

3. Be ready for typhoon season

Typhoon season runs roughly June through November. Off base, power and water outages can last longer than you're used to, and stores sell out of supplies before a storm. Set up a kit now, while shelves are full.

🔋

Portable power station

Keeps phones, a fan, and a router or lights running through an outage. Pairs well with a foldable solar panel.

See portable power stations →
🔦

LED lanterns

Safer than candles and lighter than flashlights for a whole room. Get one per bedroom.

See LED lanterns →
🚰

Collapsible water containers

Fill them before landfall; fold them flat the rest of the year.

4. Your first car in Okinawa

Most families buy a used car soon after arrival. Two inexpensive add-ons are worth it on day one.

🎥

Dash cam (front + rear)

Widely used in Japan and genuinely useful as an objective record if there's ever a fender-bender or a dispute about who was at fault.

See dash cams →
☀️

Windshield sunshade

Okinawa sun turns a parked car into an oven, even in spring. A folding shade sized for kei cars or minivans helps a lot.

See sunshades →

5. Moving money between the U.S. and Japan

Rent, utilities, and many local businesses expect yen from a Japanese bank account. If you're moving money from a U.S. account, compare your bank's wire fees and exchange rate with a service like Wise, which shows the total cost up front.

💱

Wise (international transfers)

Send dollars from a U.S. account and receive yen in a Japanese account. Always compare the final amount received, not just the fee.
